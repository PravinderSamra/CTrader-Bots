# 06 — Glossary

Every strategy-specific term used across the three transcripts, defined consistently, with the video(s) it appears in. Video key as in files 01–05: **V1** = Chart Fanatics ep. 1 with Marco Trades; **V2** = Chart Fanatics ep. 2 ("Da Vinci model"); **V3** = Inter Equity Trading.

Where the videos use different labels for the same object, the entries cross-reference each other; §7 maps standard ICT/SMC vocabulary onto the transcripts' vocabulary, and §8 lists auto-caption artifacts so future readers don't mistake them for real terms.

---

## 1. Core concepts

**Liquidity** — Resting orders in the market, above all stop losses: "liquidity is just resting orders in the market... the best example... would just again be stop losses" (V1 [3:59]). Clusters rest above highs (buy stops of trapped sellers/breakout buyers) and below lows (sell stops of trapped buyers). The organizing principle of the entire system: "Liquidity is everything for me. It gives me my direction. It gives me my bias, my entries, A to Z" (V1 [0:36], [2:39]). Used constantly in V2 and V3.

**Liquidity pool / level of liquidity** — A specific price level (or narrow zone) where such resting orders have been *confirmed* to sit. The transcripts mostly say "level of liquidity" (V2 [4:46], [57:48]; V1 [41:28]) or "a form of liquidity" (V3 [14:41]). Only levels the market has confirmed by respect-and-move-away qualify (see **Respect**). Untaken pools persist as future targets across days/weeks (V1 [25:49]–[26:28]).

**Respect (a level)** — Price approaches a prior high/low, does *not* trade through it, and then **moves away**. This is the market's confirmation that orders rest beyond the level: "if I see the market respect a high from A to B and move away, that tells me there's liquidity above that high" (V1 [13:13]). The repeated grammar: "high taken, low respecting low → liquidity" (V2 [41:10]–[41:35]; V3 [8:05]). Multiple respects strengthen the level ("respects it, respects it, respects it... it taps into it a bunch", V2 [67:20]; "taps into it once, twice, three times", V3 [13:29]). All three videos.

**Move away** — The second half of the respect test: after the approach, price must displace *away* from the level ("that's the important part. So we have liquidity at this low", V1 [20:51]–[21:32]; "once it moves away, we have built reasoning for price to push up there", V2 [67:20]). Until the move-away occurs, no liquidity is confirmed (V1 [76:22]). All three videos.

**The market communicating** — The stance that liquidity is identified reactively, never predicted: "I never like to predict where liquidity is going to be... I simply let the market communicate that to me" (V2 [5:24]; V1 [13:56]; V3 [1:16], [4:11], [14:06]: "We are not guessing. We are not predicting."). All three videos.

**Liquidity block (LB)** — A swing point that was *itself created by* sweeping liquidity and has not since been respected — therefore holds **no** resting orders beyond it. "A low that doesn't hold any liquidity" (V2 [35:05]); "a low that has spiked out previous lows... It doesn't need to get ran" (V2 [51:49]); high-side version: "a high that has taken out a previous high" (V2 [67:41]). Named in V2/V3 only; V1 describes the identical object without the name ("why would there be any liquidity above this high?... Price has not approached the high and moved away yet", V1 [28:44]–[29:25]). Used as the stop-loss anchor and as an entry zone ("anywhere below this black line is a valid buy... LB below", V3 [9:59]; V3 [12:15]). The exemption is temporary: once later respected with a move-away, it acquires liquidity like any level (V1 [76:22]). Marked as a blue box in V2/V3 (V3 [2:27]).

**Engineered liquidity** — V2's named arming condition: as price approaches the target pool, it respects it and retraces, printing a high (long case) that "respect[s] highs to the left hand side... the market telling me that we are building liquidity" (V2 [5:24]–[5:47]). "The most important part to this model: if you don't see engineered liquidity, it's not a Da Vinci model" (V2 [6:14]); "no engineered liquidity, no model" (V2 [28:11]). Also a target/partial point on the way to the external pool (V2 [58:35]). Term used verbatim in V3 ("look at the engineered liquidity we have at the highs", V3 [9:16]) and once in V1 ("we've engineered liquidity at the lows. That's where I'm going to be targeting", V1 [46:01]); V1 otherwise draws the same object as the respected high sellers trade from (V1 [21:32]–[22:46]).

**Inducement / induce** — The market's engineering of retail participation to build a pool: a BOS or reaction that makes retail enter, so their stops stack ("they work for good reason, purposely to induce liquidity into the market", V1 [8:59]; "we have just induced buyers", V2 [51:22]; "it's going to induce them to continue selling", V3 [1:16]). All three videos.

**Early buyers / early sellers** — The induced participants whose stops form the pool to be swept: "I need to see early buyers induced in the market. I need to see those early buyers taken out" (V2 [22:00]; also [48:00], [51:22]). V1/V3 use "buyers/sellers induced... trapped" for the same participants (V1 [45:24]; V3 [6:31], [7:05]).

**Trap / trap move / trap area** — The false move that sweeps the induced participants' stops, and the zone where their (doomed) entries occur. "That's going to be what I'm going to be viewing as what's called a trap" (V1 [16:17]); "this trap move to the downside" (V1 [18:27]; V3 [3:06]); "both these red boxes I'm viewing as a trap" (V1 [51:06]); "we trapped this low" (V3 [11:43]); "boom, traps again" (V2 [54:11]). Reactions inside the trap zone are "false and short-lived" (V1 [9:33]; V2 [56:59]; V3 [3:43]). All three videos. This is the phenomenon the whole doc set is named after.

**Da Vinci model** — Marco's name (V2 only) for the complete setup: left-side liquidity taken → target pool confirmed → engineered liquidity prints → early buyers induced and trapped → enter on the sweep, stop behind LB, target the pool. "I called the Da Vinci model. I kind of just threw a title on it" (V2 [2:09]–[2:34]). The name is cosmetic; V1/V3 trade the identical structure unnamed. Full rules in file 03.

**Buildup (of liquidity)** — The accumulation phase that prints the pool before the sweep: "all of this is a buildup" (V2 [41:35]); "we have what I would call a buildup of liquidity. Why is this a buildup? Lows taken... highs taken" (V2 [48:00]–[48:27]); "the first step we need to see a buildup of liquidity" (V3 [12:51]–[13:29]). Also V1's pre-open chop "building liquidity for New York to deal with" (V1 [44:44]).

**Internal / external liquidity** — External = the top/bottom of the current range (the HTF pools); internal = swing points formed inside the range on the way there. "I'm taking entries off internal liquidity taken, targeting external... typically once external is taken, there's reversals that occur" (V1 [27:37]–[28:44]). V2: "internal lows" (V2 [34:04]), "you have this engineered liquidity point... and then the external highs being up here" (V2 [60:24]). V3 expresses it as "intraday targets" vs "that higher time frame narrative" (V3 [12:51]).

**Structural liquidity** — Chains of internal swing points left behind by a trend, treated as one body of liquidity: "these are all internal lows left... it is just structural liquidity left" (V1 [64:36]); "low high low to the downside — that's structural liquidity" (V1 [59:45]); "equal highs... structural point, however you want to call it" (V1 [44:44]). V3's version: the "chain" of lows each tapping something to the left (V3 [1:52]).

**Trendline / channel liquidity** — A sloped sequence of respected highs/lows; explicitly collapsed into plain liquidity: "people can say channel, people can say trendline liquidity... it's literally just liquidity, period" (V1 [12:37]–[13:13]; also [73:42]); "you can call it a channel, you can call it structure, whatever it is, it's building liquidity" (V2 [38:23]). V1/V2.

**Equal highs / equal lows (relative equal)** — Two or more highs/lows at (approximately) the same price; an immediately markable pool: "a level of equal lows, relative equal lows... It's an area of liquidity" (V1 [41:28]); "look at this equal highs" (V2 [33:18]), "we left the equals up here" (V2 [46:24]); weekly equal highs as the macro draw (V3 [0:43]–[1:16]); "relative equals also 4-hour high" (V1 [83:36]). All three videos.

**Liquidity void** — A stretch of one-directional movement leaving no swing points, hence nothing to mark and no reason to trade. The host's term (V1 [15:44]–[16:17]); Marco's equivalent statement: "all I can mark on is we've taken out this high, period. I don't see any other liquidity. So why would I be trading?" (V1 [14:32]–[15:06]). V1 only.

**Fractal** — The model repeats identically at every timeframe: "it's fractal... this could happen on the daily, it could happen all the way down to the one minute" (V1 [12:06]); "it's all fractal all the way down to the 5-second if you want" (V2 [41:10]); HTF instance gives direction, LTF instance gives entry (V2 [23:01], [39:32]; V3 [15:11]). All three videos.

**Direction / bias / draw** — The side of the market you are permitted to trade, defined by the nearest logical untaken pool: "your eyes should be back to these highs... the only logical liquidity point I can see in front of us" (V3 [3:06]); "liquidity... gives me my direction, it gives me my bias" (V1 [2:39]). The transcripts never use ICT's phrase "draw on liquidity"; they say "targeting liquidity" / "a reason for price to push" (V2 [67:20]). All three videos.

**Counter-bias sell (short-term sell)** — A trade taken against the HTF bias into the near-side pool, whose completion can arm the with-bias trade: "this was essentially what I would call a counter-bias sell, short-term sell" (V2 [29:07]). V2 only.

**Confluence** — Any independent factor agreeing with the setup (trapped buyers, LB at the extreme, timing, HTF alignment): "that right away is a massive confluence for me" (V1 [73:05]); "it is a great confluence... because now price has a reason to go lower" (V2 [68:51]). V1/V2.

## 2. Price-action verbs (all synonyms for trading through a level)

The transcripts use these interchangeably for one event — price trading beyond an identified level and consuming the orders there. A bot needs exactly one definition (see file 08); a reader needs to recognize all of them:

- **Sweep / swept out** — "we swept liquidity" (V1 [16:17]); "sweeps out this low" (V2 [22:00]). All three videos.
- **Spike / spiked out** — "we've spiked out all of Asia liquidity" (V1 [43:25]); "a low that has spiked out previous lows" (V2 [51:49]). V1/V2/V3.
- **Stab / stabbed out** — "your entry as soon as the low gets stabbed" (V2 [39:09]); "as soon as price stabs out this low" (V3 [9:59]); "Lows stabbed out post NYC" (V1 [83:36]).
- **Run / ran** — "we run that last level of liquidity" (V2 [38:46]); "why would we run this low?" (V3 [5:24]).
- **Take / taken out** — "once this low right here is taken... you enter your buy position" (V2 [14:11]); "high's taken" (V1 [107:47]).
- **Grab** — "we grabbed something from the left-hand side" (V2 [3:58]); "grabs what? The engineered liquidity" (V2 [50:03]).
- **Tap / tapped into** — near-synonym with a softer sense: touching *into* a level or zone (especially an LB) rather than through a pool: "this wick right here tapped into something on the left hand side" (V3 [1:52]); "we tap into these areas, take entries off them" (V3 [12:15]); "taps into it a bunch" (V2 [67:20]).
- **Clear / cleared** — purpose-language for a sweep at scale: "What was the purpose of it?... To clear all of this liquidity" (V3 [2:27]).
- **Hunted** — "we've hunted all of this liquidity" (V1 [83:36]). The host also uses "stop-loss hunt" for the retail label of the same event (V1 [7:46]).

Related recognition vocabulary: **false move / false reaction / false push** — a reaction or spike that exists only to induce/trap ("this false move to the upside... we take out that seller liquidity and then the move occurs", V1 [21:32]–[22:46]; "deem them to be false", V2 [37:58]; V3 [3:43]); **wick** — the candle shadow a sweep typically leaves (V1 [60:20], [97:15]); **extreme** — the outermost point of the swept structure / the range ("if you trail your eyes all the way to the extreme", V2 [38:46]; "tap into an LB at the extreme", V3 [2:27]; "the low of New York... the extreme", V1 [86:59]).

## 3. Chart markings (color conventions)

**Red box** — In all three videos: a retail-entry / trap zone (POI, OB, imbalance area, respected-highs area) where reactions are expected to be false, *not* a level to trade from: "I mark it on as a red box, not an area I'd be trading from" (V2 [37:58]); "both these red boxes I'm viewing as a trap" (V1 [51:06]); buildup zones (V3 [7:05], [13:29]).

**Blue box / blue line** — In V2/V3: a liquidity block ("we can plot on a blue box", V3 [2:27]; V3 [3:43]) or an identified liquidity level ("plot on a blue line. That is a form of liquidity for us", V3 [13:29]; "all these blue lines are essentially targets", V1 [89:36]). **Caution:** in V1's whiteboard segment the "blue box" is the *trap zone above the lows* ("if there is reactions in this blue box... I understand it to be false", V1 [9:33]) — the color conventions are NOT consistent between V1 and V2/V3. See file 07.

**Purple box** — V1 only: the Asia session range ("this is Asia high and Asia low. Just the purple box", V1 [44:03]).

**Black line** — V3: the liquidity level whose stab triggers entry ("anywhere below this black line I have marked on is a valid buy", V3 [9:59]).

**Dollar sign ($)** — V2 whiteboard shorthand for "orders resting here" (V2 [5:47]).

## 4. Execution and trade-management terms

**Entry on the stab** — The base trigger: enter the moment the identified pool is traded through, no further confirmation (V2 [14:11]; V1 [30:34]; V3 [5:56]). See file 03 §2.

**Over-refine** — Marco's pejorative for demanding extra LTF confirmation (an imbalance, an exact POI) inside the swept low before entering: "a lot of people like to overrefine and look for an imbalance in this low. In my opinion, unnecessary... you're going to start missing entries" (V2 [13:46]–[14:35], [48:50]). V2 only (concept echoed V1 [67:52]: "I don't like to refine it too much... It's unnecessary in my opinion").

**Refine / refinement** — The legitimate version: dropping to a lower timeframe to find a *smaller LB / fractal setup* when the HTF stop is too wide ("another liquidity block, a refined area right here... that shrinks our SL quite a bit", V3 [8:41]; "when you go into the lower time frames and apply refinements, that's when the RRs can drastically increase", V3 [15:54]; V2 [36:39]–[39:32]). V2/V3.

**Limit entry (passive variant)** — Resting a limit order at the liquidity level with the stop beyond the LB, for HTF/CFD setups: "you set a limit at this liquidity point... stop loss goes below, you don't even need to look at the chart" (V2 [58:10]; his USDJPY trade, V2 [68:09]). Marco personally market-executes on futures (V1 [46:33]).

**Tagged in** — Getting filled on such an order/level: "you get tagged in perfectly at that LB" (V3 [12:51]); "limits don't get tagged sometimes" on forex spread (V1 [46:33]).

**Stop covers the low/high** — The stop-placement idiom: the stop sits *beyond* the left-hand-side swing/LB so the whole structure is inside the stop: "your stop loss is going to cover this low from the left-hand side" (V2 [16:17]); "stop covers this high always" (V1 [69:02]); "stop covering liquidity block" (V3 [14:41]).

**Breathing room** — Extra stop buffer required on CFD/forex because of spread and broker-feed variance: "this is going to be on a CFD chart — let's give it a little bit of breathing room" (V2 [39:09]–[39:32]; V1 [68:28]). Futures need only "a tick or two" (V1 [69:02]).

**Roll the stop** — Moving the stop in the trade's favor as structure prints: "I roll my stop to break even" (V2 [63:59]); "I'll look to trail my stop below this low" (V1 [32:17]); "as soon as price takes out these highs, roll your stop loss" (V3 [9:59]). **BE / break even** — stop at entry price (V2 [63:59]; V3 [6:31]; V1 [98:46]).

**Partial** — Closing part of the position at an intermediate liquidity level (never at a fixed R): V1 [47:05] (50–70%); V2 [64:19] (20–25%, prefers holding); V3 [12:51] (intraday targets). **Runner** — the remainder held to the HTF target (V2 [43:33], host).

**Scale-in / adding volume** — Adding contracts where the model independently re-triggers below a fresh local sweep (V1 live session, [89:36]–[92:05]); each add has its own invalidation (V1 [92:05]). V1 only.

**RR (risk-to-reward)** — Reward distance divided by stop distance; Marco's floor is 1:3 (V2 [36:18]); "when the stop loss is too big, the RR is shot" (V2 [35:05]).

**Invalidated** — Setup or trade voided: by the stop ("pullback and a continuation. Boom. Trade invalidated", V2 [26:14]) or per-scale-in level ("or else this scale-in is invalid", V1 [92:05]).

**Futures units** (V1 live session): **tick** — minimum futures price increment ("320 ticks away", V1 [85:11]; "140-tick move", V1 [94:38]); **micros** — micro futures contracts ("only got five micros on currently", V1 [85:11]); **cons** — contracts ("taking off another two cons here", V1 [106:46]).

## 5. Session, timing, and news terms

**Stock open** — 9:30 a.m. New York, marked on every intraday chart as "an important level of time" (V1 [42:40]–[43:25], [65:14], [73:05]). V1 only.

**Sessions** — **Asia** (range marked as a box; its highs/lows are session liquidity, V1 [43:25]); **London / Frankfurt** (moves outside Marco's window, V1 [48:24], [49:30]; where his UJ limit filled while asleep, V2 [68:09]); **New York** (his trading window, V1 [42:02], [49:30]); **NY lunch** — the dead-volume hour to be flat before ("typically the hour tends to be dead in volume", V1 [105:56]).

**Time window** — The fixed daily session outside which nothing matters: "I have a specific time window... what happens outside of the time window is completely irrelevant to me" (V1 [49:30]). V1 only.

**News / red-folder news** — High-impact calendar events; never enter before, wait ~2–4 minutes after release (V1 [67:13]: "some sort of red folded news event" — caption artifact for *red-folder*). Liquidity is often built the day before news (V1 [26:28]). V1 only.

**Timeframe close (timing confluence)** — Candle closes (5m/15m/30m/1H/4H) used as timing evidence for entries/adds: "the 4-hour closed, spiked it right into my area... at 10:00 a.m." (V1 [83:36]); "you need like a heavy timing confluence in order to take these kind of entries" (V1 [86:59]). V1 only.

**PDL (previous day low)** — Prior day's session low, used as a stop reference: "that's why I got my stop below PDL right now" (V1 [84:30]). V1 only.

**PA** — price action (V1 [42:40]; V2 [46:24]; V3 passim). **HTF / LTF** — higher/lower timeframe(s).

## 6. Retail-concept vocabulary (used as a map of where others get trapped, never traded directly)

These appear throughout as the things retail enters on — i.e., where pools get built (V1 [8:59]: "these retail concepts are all used to build liquidity in the market"; kept only because "they aid me in understanding where the liquidity is", V1 [37:58]):

- **BOS (break of structure)** — retail's momentum signal when a high/low is taken; the inducement trigger (V1 [4:36], [10:12], [43:25]; V2 [13:20]; V3 [7:05] in substance). Captions corrupt it to "boss"/"VOS" (V1 [73:42]; V2 [0:46]).
- **Market structure shift** — same family: "they're going to be looking at this as a BOS, okay? Market structure shift, whatever they want to call it" (V1 [73:42]).
- **Order block (OB)** — retail POI inside the trap zone (V1 [5:10], [8:19], [55:08]; V2 [10:57]).
- **POI (point of interest)** — generic retail entry area (V1 [8:19]; V2 [10:57], [56:38]).
- **Fair value gap (FVG) / imbalance / inefficiency** — unfilled-gap concepts; marked only inside trap zones or noted below lows (V1 [5:10], [55:08], [58:09], [64:36] "massive inefficiency"; V2 [13:46] — entering off one is "over-refining").
- **Breaker structure** — retail reversal pattern (V1 [5:10], [7:08]).
- **Fibonacci / 50% of the range / discount** — retracement-level concepts (V1 [4:36], [15:06], [54:32]–[55:08]; V2 [53:26]; "your discount or whatever — I don't view it like that", V2 [52:41]).
- **Support / resistance, supply / demand, structure trading, smart money concepts (SMC), ICT** — named as the frameworks whose users supply the liquidity (V1 [8:59], [22:46]–[23:20], [78:39]; V2 [53:26]).
- **SMT divergence / cross-market correlation** — mentioned and explicitly not used (V1 [63:28]–[64:04]).
- **Level two data / the tape** — order-book data; host's aside that futures could verify resting orders (V1 [55:08]). Not part of the system.

## 10. New and refined terms from the 2026 sources (official playbook + Inter Equity channel)

Added 2026-07-22 from `../04-official-playbook/marco-trades-liquidity-playbook.md` (PB) and the 20 Inter Equity Trading transcripts (`(IET YYYY-MM-DD "title" [MM:SS])` → `../01-transcripts/inter-equity-channel/`; timestamps are inline-tag video time, see file 09 §0). Full treatment in files 09–10.

**No man's land** — The choppy region between the marked pool above and the marked pool below; where liquidity *builds*; explicitly not traded: "I call this like no man's land... I'm typically not trading in here. I want to see price either above or below. I will wait days... or weeks" (IET 2026-05-13 [02:05]–[02:25]); "everything in between here is now just going to be known as noise" (IET 2026-07-21 [12:07]). See file 10 P10.

**Early traders** — Umbrella for early buyers *and* early sellers (file 06 §1), now carried by a written rule: "**we must only trade once the early traders are trapped. No ifs, ands, or buts**" (IET 2026-06-09 [07:05]–[09:05]).

**"No liquidity block, no entry" / "no LB, no stop loss"** — The LB requirement stated as a hard gate: the LB's whole job is to supply the stop level, and without one there is no trade even when everything else aligns (IET 2026-03-10 [04:52]–[05:12]; IET 2026-07-14 [07:55]–[08:15]). Refines the LB entry (file 06 §1, file 03 §1.5).

**Imbalance (as LB geometry)** — IET equates the sweep candle's imbalance with the liquidity block zone: "leaving this imbalance area, otherwise known as a liquidity block" (IET 2026-03-03 [04:02]); limits are parked "right in the imbalance" ([07:12]). Distinct from §6's retail-FVG usage — same word, two roles; role is determined by whether the gap sits beyond a sweep (LB) or inside a trap zone (retail POI).

**Structure liquidity (formal definition)** — "Liquidity below higher lows... liquidity above lower highs. That's why we call it structure liquidity" (IET 2026-06-09 [05:05]–[05:45]). Formalizes §1's structural-liquidity entry.

**H4-candle model / the 6–10 a.m. candle** — The New-York H4 candle spanning 6:00–10:00 a.m.; its low/high are the model's reference levels (IET 2026-02-10 [01:27]–[03:27]). See file 09 §3.5.

**10:00 a.m. reversal** — The new-H4-candle-open trap-and-reverse: the pre-10:00 sell-off (bullish case) below the prior H4 low is "viewed... being false", and "once we trade below previous 4-hour low... longs are active" (IET 2026-02-10 [02:47]–[03:27], [07:47]–[08:07]). Related: stock-open moves are presumed trap moves (IET 2026-04-28 [08:43]).

**Inside day** — Day trading inside the previous day's high/low; expected after a both-extremes ("outside") day and treated as a reduced/no-trade day (IET 2026-07-07 [02:43]–[03:23]).

**PDH / PDL frame ("daily-to-daily")** — Previous-day-high/low used as target/arming levels; day-boundary sweep pairs ("Monday spikes above Friday['s high] → target Friday's low", IET 2026-07-14 [17:36]); the PDL-spike-holding-an-older-LB long script (IET 2026-07-07 [07:23]–[08:03]). Extends §5's PDL entry (V1 used PDL only as a stop reference).

**Ping-pong / trading both sides** — Trading the trap leg and the reversal leg of the same day as two independent full setups chained at the target sweep (IET 2026-04-28, whole video; file 09 §4).

**Stacking entries** — Sequential full entries in one direction, each a complete setup, justified while the shared target survives: "if our target is left intact, all that means is we have another opportunity to sell" (IET 2026-05-05 [08:13]). Distinct from V1's in-trade scale-ins (§4). See file 09 §5.

**Target intact** — The standing validity condition for a bias/stack: "as long as the highs stay intact, whatever happens below this high, buys are valid. Cuz that's the target" (IET 2026-06-30 [04:04]); "once that target's taken, the sell is invalid" (IET 2026-04-07 [03:02]).

**Lower probability (graded lockout)** — IET's softening of the V1/PB absolute lockout: against-sequence trades are "automatically deemed lower probability" and permitted only with time confluence (IET 2026-04-28 [01:02]–[01:22]; IET 2026-07-14 [12:36]).

**Risk-free / risk off** — Position state after rolling the stop to entry (or better) once a liquidity level is consumed in the trade's favor: "once we took out this internal high, I went risk-free" (IET 2026-06-30 [01:23]). Partial at that level only if its RR justifies it (~1:5–1:6+); otherwise BE only (IET 2026-07-14 [20:18]–[20:58]). Note PB p.03's stricter "no break-even stops unless partials have been taken" — see file 05 §2.6 item 1.

**Trail-to-LB** — Rolling the stop behind a freshly-formed liquidity block: "we've created some sort of an LB here... This LB should protect my SL. If it runs it, I don't want to be in the trade no more" (IET 2026-06-30 [10:08]–[10:28]).

**Sniper entry / 4-candle model** — The candle-anatomy entry: candle 2's sweep-wick forms the LB, candle 3 leaves a low, candle 4's spike below it is the trigger, stop beyond the candle-2 wick (IET 2026-03-24; file 10 P7).

**Liquidity run / false run** — Additional synonyms for the trap sweep: "you can call it a trap move, you can call it a liquidity run, a false run, whatever you want to call it" (IET 2026-03-17 [03:04]). Extends §2's synonym list. Also **"falling knife"** — how a valid per-system entry can feel when catching the stab (IET 2026-07-21 [14:09]–[14:29]).

**Squeezed(-up) price action** — Chart state where highs/lows are too compressed to read; the fix is dropping one timeframe: "when price action is very squeezed like this, I can't really understand what's going on... just go down a time frame" (IET 2026-02-24 [01:42]; IET 2026-03-17 [02:24]).

**Bank-holiday rule** — "If there ever is a bank holiday, do not trade, guys. Do not trade" (IET 2026-07-14 [09:56]–[10:16]).

**Training the eyes (series)** — Now also the name of IET's recurring recognition-drill series (Ep. 12: 2026-01-31; Ep. 13: 2026-02-17), distilled into the pattern library of file 10; the prescribed practice is replay-tool backtesting "to train your eyes. Without this, you will struggle to see setups in the live market" (IET 2026-02-03 [02:07]–[02:47]).
