Why it should work

US stock indices have tended to rise in the 24 hours before a scheduled Federal Reserve (FOMC) announcement (Lucca & Moench, Journal of Finance 2015). The usual explanation is a reward for holding stocks into a big, uncertain event. Of all the academic calendar effects we tested, it is the only one still profitable after FTMO's costs.

The rules

  • Markets: US100.cash and US500.cash.
  • Buy at 2:00pm New York time on the first day of each scheduled Fed meeting (the day before the statement).
  • Sell at 1:58pm New York time on announcement day, just before the 2:00pm statement — it never holds through the announcement.
  • Safety stop 3% below the entry. The worst event since 2014 was −1.9%.
  • Scheduled meetings only. The dates for 2021–2027 are built into the bot's settings.

Size

  • $20,000 notional per index. A typical event moves each position by about ±$150.
  • The worst event since 2014 would have cost about −$380 per index (about −$760 for both).

Running it

  • Attach FOMC_Drift.cs to a US100.cash, 1-hour chart on the FTMO account. Leave the defaults.
  • First live trade: 27 October 2026 (sold on 28 October before the statement).
  • Once a year, add the next year's meeting day-1 dates from the Fed's calendar (federalreserve.gov → Monetary Policy → FOMC calendars) into the "Extra Dates" setting.

Switch-off rule (monthly review)

Pause the bot if its last 16 trades (about one year) are net negative, or if a single event loses more than 3%. It is small and has been weakening on US500, so it is an add-on, not an income engine.
