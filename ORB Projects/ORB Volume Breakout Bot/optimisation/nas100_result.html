The one thing that is drifting

Per-trade profit falls year on year — +$27.75, +$15.77, +$27.67, +$15.55, +$11.07 — and 2026 is the weakest and most outlier-dependent. The obvious suspect was the stop: a fixed 60 points has quietly stopped meaning the same thing. That turned out to be measurable, and wrong — see the section after this one.

The range doubled, but only because the index did

Daily range in points roughly doubled, 106 to 211. As a share of price it barely moved — 0.61% to 0.98%, no trend. So NAS100 is not becoming a wilder market; it is becoming a bigger number.

Which makes the fixed stop a moving target

60 points was 62% of a typical day's range in 2023 and is 28% today. That is not one strategy tested over five years — it is a stop that has been shrinking in real terms the whole time, and it lines up with the falling per-trade profit.

And 60 was fitted to 2024's volatility

60 points is 51% of 2024's median range — the year it was chosen on. It was never a universal number, it was that year's number. Holding it fixed exports one year's volatility to every other year.

The obvious fix was tested, and it failed

If the drifting stop were the problem, pinning it to a constant share of the day's range should have flattened the decline. All five years were re-run with the stop set to 50% of the ORB range instead of a flat 60 points, entries untouched. It was worse in four years of five.

The wider stop worked exactly as intended, and still lost

Win rate rose from 38.5% to 47.2% — a bigger stop really is hit less often. But the average win fell from $208.98 to $111.37. Risk is fixed in dollars, so a wider stop buys a smaller position, and the same price move simply earns less. Fewer losses did not pay for smaller wins.

So the drift is real but not the problem

60 points does mean something different now than in 2023. Correcting it costs more than it returns. The falling per-trade profit has some other cause, and remains unexplained.

What to do next

The configuration that has now survived five years and one serious challenge is the one to stop adjusting. Further tuning has a poor record here.

  1. Leave the stop at 60 points

    It has been tested against the best alternative anyone could motivate from the data, and it won in four years out of five.

  2. Confirm the risk reduction on the other four years

    On 2026 alone, halving remaining risk at 1R turned +$553 into +$844 without touching the average win. That is the one open improvement worth settling, and it is a single run per year.

  3. Then forward-test on demo

    Everything here is simulated fills on historical bars. Several weeks unattended against live spreads is the only evidence that has not been collected yet, and it is the only kind that cannot be curve-fitted.

What would still change the picture. 2026 is the weakest year, the most dependent on a handful of winners, and it is only eight months long — strip its top five trades and it is $1,434 down. If that is the start of decay rather than the stop drifting out of range, the next full year will show it. Treat the equity curve as a reason to keep going carefully, not as a settled result.
