Two findings that change the plan

Entering at the bell is the losing half — do not “fix” the clock to 09:31

With three times the earlier sample the two clock states now separate clearly. The accidental 10:31 ET entries made £2,486 at PF 1.12 across 303 trades; the 09:31 ET entries lost £1,700 at PF 0.83 across 129. This matches the earlier M5 study in this repository, which found waiting until 10:00 ET to be the single most important rule. Make the clock deterministic — then set entry deliberately at 10:00–10:30 ET, not at the open.

All the profit is on wide-range days — because the stop cannot see volatility

Split by the day’s ORB range, the three narrower quartiles lose £1,613, £3,231 and £1,844. The widest quartile makes £7,475. A fixed 50pt stop is 45% of a tight day’s range but only 13% of a wide one — on quiet days the 2R target is simply out of reach before the stop is hit.

Next test: let the stop scale with the day

The range finding argues for changing the risk unit, not adding a filter. Filtering out quiet days throws away two thirds of the sample and makes trade frequency depend on the volatility regime. Scaling the stop keeps every trade and fixes the actual defect — and the bot already supports it.

  1. Turn off the fixed-point stop

    EnableFixedPointStop = false switches the bot to StopLossOrbPercent, which anchors the stop to a percentage of that day’s ORB range. No code change needed — it is existing, already-tested behaviour.

  2. Run 15%, 20% and 25% across all five years

    At 15% a 373pt day gets a 56pt stop, close to today’s 50pt, while a 110pt day gets 17pt instead of 50pt. That is the whole point: quiet days become tradeable rather than being donations. Watch for stops so tight that spread and M1 noise take them out.

  3. Judge it on the body, not the total

    The question is not whether net P&L rises. It is whether the median trade improves and whether the result survives removing the top five. A configuration that only wins on its tail has not been fixed.

  4. Then fix the clock — and move entry later, not earlier

    Two timezones, London for the range start and New York for the bell, then test entry at 10:00 and 10:30 ET against the current accidental 10:31.

A caution about this round. The 1.2× volume filter and the 2R breakeven were chosen after looking at all five years of the previous run, so no year is fully clean any more. The five-year total is now a weakly in-sample number, not an out-of-sample one. The findings that survive that caveat are the mechanical ones — the clock split, the range-versus-stop relationship, the outlier concentration — because those describe the shape of the trade book rather than the level of its profit.
