The breakeven stop cuts the right tail

44 trades closed between £0 and £20 — breakeven exits worth £113 in total. Every one had already moved 0.6R in the right direction. A system that needs big winners is scratching a third of its candidates for nothing while losses stay full size.

The split is not chance

A permutation test over all 141 trades returns p < 0.00001. Drawing 84 trades from the in‑sample edge reproduced a result as poor as the real out‑of‑sample one in zero of 100,000 simulations. Bootstrapping the out‑of‑sample edge instead, 2.9% of 30‑trade years finish positive.

What to change, in order

The structure may still be sound — earlier M5 research in this repository found a real edge in this family. What follows targets the implementation, cheapest and highest‑leverage first.

  1. Raise or remove the breakeven trigger

    0.6R is far too early for a system with no take‑profit that lives entirely off its runners. Test breakeven off, at 1.5R, and at 2R. The 44 scratched trades are where the missing money is.

  2. Drop the volume filter to 1.2–1.3×

    Not for edge — for sample size. At 1.4× the filter rejected a breakout on 315 days against 94 that traded. Eleven to thirty‑seven trades a year cannot support any conclusion, let alone an optimisation.

  3. Enable shorts

    Doubles the sample immediately and removes a directional bet that is currently being made by omission rather than by choice.

  4. Fix the session clock

    Fixed UTC times put entry 61 minutes after the opening bell on 65% of trading days. This does not by itself explain the losses — both clock states were profitable — but until it is fixed the bot has two definitions and neither can be measured.

  5. Then optimise, walk‑forward

    Choose parameters on 2022–2023, test once on 2024, and keep 2025–2026 out of the decision entirely. Roughly 96 parameters against 141 trades is why the first attempt produced a curve rather than an edge.

On deploying this live. Nothing here says the idea cannot work. It says this configuration has not been shown to work: every year that was not used to build it lost money, and the profitable stretch is a description of the search that produced it. The honest expectation from the out‑of‑sample record is −£29.53 per trade.
