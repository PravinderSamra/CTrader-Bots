01 Thesis — where the edge comes from

CRT is Wyckoff's spring/upthrust condensed onto a single higher-timeframe candle. The tradable event is not the breakout — it is the failed breakout.

Resting stop and breakout orders cluster just beyond the high and low of significant candles. A higher-timeframe extreme (an H4 or H1 high/low) sits on a deeper pool of that resting liquidity than any M1 level does. Price is drawn to run that pool; if the run immediately fails — price sweeps beyond the extreme and closes back inside the range — it signals the breakout had no continuation and the traders who triggered are now trapped. Their forced exit fuels the reverse move toward the opposite side of the range.

Three things convert that observation into an actual edge, and each is independently testable rather than assumed:

Lever 1

Deep liquidity anchor

Fading H4/H1 extremes fades the levels most likely to hold real resting orders — not arbitrary M1 noise.

Lever 2

Tight structural stop

M1 entry places the stop just beyond the sweep extreme. Small stop against a range-width target = high reward:risk. This is the real profit engine, not hit rate.

Lever 3

Time conditioning

The sweep (the "Judas swing") disproportionately fires at the London and New York opens, when liquidity is injected. Conditioning on killzones lifts the base rate.

The backtest exists to measure whether these levers survive realistic costs on gold — not to confirm a belief.

03 Definitions & notation

All levels derive from the most recent closed candle on the range timeframe (R). Resample H1/H4 from the same M1 series so levels are exact.

# Range candle R = last CLOSED bar on TF_R (H4 or H1) RH = high(R) # range high RL = low(R) # range low RNG = RH - RL # range width EQ = (RH + RL) / 2 # equilibrium (0.50) Q25 = RL + 0.25*RNG Q75 = RL + 0.75*RNG # δ = minimum sweep penetration (noise filter) delta = max( min_ticks , k * ATR_M1(14) ) # Killzones (UK local; shift for BST/GMT) KZ_LONDON = 07:00–10:00 KZ_NEWYORK = 13:30–16:30

Sweep (long side): an M1 low prints ≤ RL − delta. Return-inside: a later M1 bar closes > RL within W M1 bars of the sweep extreme.

04 Entry logic — long (mirror for short)

  1. Define the range.On each new closed R, compute RH, RL, RNG, EQ, Q25, Q75. Skip if RNG < 0.5 × ATR_H4(14) — a dead range has no fuel.
  2. Arm on a killzone sweep.Wait for an M1 low ≤ RL − delta occurring inside a killzone. Record the sweep extreme SL_ext.
  3. Confirm the failure.Model A — first M1 close back above RL within W bars. Model B — M1 market-structure shift after the sweep (close above the last M1 swing high formed during the sweep). Test both.
  4. Enter.One of: market-on-confirm · limit at RL · limit at Q25 · fill of the M1 displacement FVG. Parameter entry_model.
  5. Place the stop.SL = SL_ext − buffer, buffer = 0.25 × ATR_M1(14).
  6. Set targets.T1 = EQ (scale out p%), T2 = RH. Alt single target = Q75. Parameter target_model.
  7. Manage & cap.Optional break-even after T1. Time-stop: exit if neither T1 nor SL hit within M minutes. Max one trade per R per direction — no re-entry after invalidation on the same range candle.

06 Backtest validity — non-negotiable

These are where a CRT backtest silently lies to you. Enforce all of them.

Causality

Detect the sweep and the return-inside bar-by-bar using only past and current M1 data. Never inspect the completed forming-candle's full range — that is look-ahead. Iterate M1 forward and act on closes.

Data & time

Single continuous M1 XAUUSD feed; derive H1/H4 by resampling it. Pin one exchange timezone and handle DST explicitly, or the killzone filter drifts an hour twice a year. Handle rollover and thin-liquidity hours.

Costs — this is what kills M1 gold

Cost realism decides the result. Gold on M1 carries a meaningful spread-bet spread and the entry fires right after a fast sweep, so slippage is adverse exactly when you transact. Model spread + commission + entry_slippage as parameters, and require the edge to survive a 2× cost stress.

07 Acceptance gates — how to know it's real

A positive equity curve is not evidence. The strategy earns the label "edge" only if it clears every gate below. If it fails a gate, the honest outcome is to drop the complexity that gate tests — not to re-optimise around it.

Random-entry null. Beat the 95th percentile of a Monte-Carlo null (≥1,000 runs) using identical sessions and stop/target geometry with random entry times.
Direction test. Sweep-continuation (enter with the breakout) must perform worse — proving reversion is the signal, not just volatility harvesting.
Session ablation. Turning the killzone filter off must degrade performance. If it doesn't, drop the filter.
Out-of-sample. Walk-forward or a strict 60/40 split; OOS expectancy stays positive after costs.
Cost stress. Edge survives 2× modelled costs.
Multiple-testing haircut. Report combinations tried; apply a deflated-Sharpe / SPA adjustment or reserve one untouched final hold-out.

09 Known failure modes

Expect the edge to be modest, conditional, and concentrated. It will most likely break via: cost drag from M1 gold spreads; false sweeps in chop; whipsaw when RNG is small; and news-driven sweeps that do not revert (genuine continuation). If results look spectacular, suspect look-ahead in sweep detection or an unmodelled cost before you believe them.
